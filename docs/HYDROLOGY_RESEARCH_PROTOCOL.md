# Forecast-driven catchment memory: frozen research protocol

## Research question and scope

Can a small forecast-driven catchment-memory model improve daily high-discharge warnings at historically gauged locations excluded from model fitting? This is a new experiment, not an amendment to the flood-stage study. A high-discharge percentile is not a flood stage, inundated road, worker exposure, or validated safety threshold. No model from this study enters production.

The design review challenges three assumptions: that a larger architecture is necessarily better, that current flood thresholds establish historical labels, and that a final archive proves historical availability. The user authorized autonomous research. Tests will cover the public acquisition/quality parser, causal feature builder, split validator, budget threshold selector, and paired evaluation. Fictional test fixtures never enter experiment data.

The literature review covered Caravan MultiMet, regionalized LSTM modeling in ungauged basins, mass-conserving LSTMs, Google's global streamflow study implementation, NeuralHydrology, and NOAA NWM. Source URLs, retrieval times and hashes are retained under the experiment's research directory. The proposed contribution is an evaluated, reproducible forecast-memory fusion experiment, not a claim to have invented hydrological reservoirs or a new state-of-the-art architecture.

## Population and target

The registry in `experiments/sprint-20261017-hydrology/stations.json` contains 96 US CAMELS gauges selected from metadata before collecting their outcomes. Select the lowest station identifier per HUC4, then the first 60 development, 18 validation and 18 test HUC4s ordered by SHA-256 of HUC4. Require a stream station, a known HUC, area, elevation, and a contiguous-US time zone. Test HUC2s are 17 and 18; geographic validation uses 03 and 12; all other selected HUC2s are development. This keeps Columbia/Pacific Northwest and California drainage systems out of fitting and keeps the connected Mississippi system on one side. One gauge per HUC4 reduces nested-gauge duplication. HUCs are drainage screening metadata, not an audited river-network graph; interbasin transfers remain a limitation.

Acquire USGS daily mean discharge, parameter 00060/statistic 00003, in cubic feet per second, from January 1, 2000 to September 30, 2024. Require exactly one matching series, identifier and agency. Use only finite, nonnegative values with the exact quality code `A`. Exclude provisional, estimated, ice-affected, absent and ambiguous values. A gauge requires 3,000 accepted reference days before 2016. The target is a daily mean strictly above that gauge's 95th percentile of accepted 2000-2015 values. This reference never uses later labels. Missing days do not become negatives. Record exclusions without substituting gauges.

The geographic test is model transfer to historically gauged watersheds, not prediction in truly ungauged basins: target normalization and lagged discharge require each gauge's earlier history. These observations and labels have not been used in the earlier PlanIT county/wind studies. CAMELS gauges mostly represent minimally disturbed basins, so transfer to regulated utility-site rivers is unproven.

Issue decisions at 00:00 UTC on day D. Primary targets are local-calendar daily means on D+1; a separately fitted secondary model targets D+2. Report actual hours to the beginning, midpoint and end of each local day, including DST. These are daily-mean warnings, not instantaneous crossing times. Do not describe a daily mean as the day's maximum or infer an exact crossing hour.

## Forecasts, memory and availability

Use Caravan MultiMet v1.1 ECMWF IFS HRES basin-averaged precipitation and temperature. The decoded archive spans January 1, 2016 to September 30, 2024, with leads 1 through 10. For issue day I, lead 1 covers UTC day I and lead 2 covers I+1. At decision D use the run dated D-1, a full-day dissemination buffer, and leads 2 through 5. Include all four forecast days so the model can account for the UTC/local daily offset without treating UTC daily rainfall as a measured local-day total. Temperature and rainfall are forecasts, not realized weather.

Build antecedent states from lead-1 forecasts dated at most D-2, so every state input also has a full-day buffer. Use causal exponential precipitation memories with 2, 7, 30 and 90-day time constants. Compare those with a degree-day snow partition: store precipitation when forecast temperature is at or below zero; otherwise melt at most 3 mm per positive degree C per day, bounded by stored snow. Apply the same reservoir bank to liquid precipitation plus melt. This conserves the proxy snow store, not the catchment water balance. It omits evapotranspiration, groundwater routing and observed snow. Reservoirs are parallel feature filters, not additive physical stores. No fitted hydrological constants or test-derived parameters.

Hydrology features use accepted discharge from local dates D-3, D-4, D-7 and D-14, normalized by the fixed reference threshold, plus a recent-change feature. The latest daily mean ends about 40-44 hours before the decision. Reject a row if a required input is missing; use one common complete-row population for every ablation. This excludes more winter and discontinued-gauge dates; report coverage by role, year and gauge.

**Availability limitation:** MultiMet explicitly omits dissemination delays, and today's USGS archive includes revisions. A 24-hour forecast buffer and three-calendar-day discharge lag are conservative research assumptions, not per-record proof of historical receipt. The scientific experiment is a reconstructed-input forecast comparison, conditional on those assumptions. It cannot certify an as-issued operational replay or deployment readiness. Do not feed future observations or reanalysis as forecasts. Do not silently substitute missing data. A separate prospective logging study is required before operational claims.

Do not use GraphCast reforecasts, NWM retrospectives, reprocessed satellite scenes or nominal-latency satellite features as historically available operational forecasts. The existing satellite pipeline has unresolved basin alignment, pagination and publication-time issues. No satellite ablation is justified without repairing and independently certifying that contract. No advisory Jev image calls are needed.

## Splits, recipes and selection

Allow a 2016 state warm-up. Fit on development gauges with target dates in 2017-2020. Calibrate on development gauges in 2021. Select on development and geographic-validation gauges in 2022, with equal weight for their APs. Exclude the first and last seven days of each calendar-year development/calibration/selection boundary. Final forward and geographic tests run January 8, 2023 through September 28, 2024. Do not fit or select using final labels. Geographic-test labels are never eligible for model, calibration or threshold fitting. Earlier-year reference discharge at those gauges defines the target and inputs only.

Compare calibrated lagged-flow persistence, seasonal climatology, forecast-rainfall ranking, and these fixed recipes: standardized logistic regression, XGBoost, and LightGBM on weather-only, hydrology-only and weather+hydrology features. LightGBM also tests weather+hydrology+precipitation-memory and weather+hydrology+snow-aware-memory. These paired ablations isolate hydrology, antecedent memory and snow partition. Weather includes current run precipitation/temperature leads 2-5, season sine/cosine, latitude, longitude, log area and elevation. Hydrology includes season/static features. No station ID or HUC enters a model.

Use seed 42, logistic C=1/max_iter=2000, boosting 200 estimators/learning_rate=.04/max_depth=3; XGBoost subsample=.8/colsample_bytree=.9, LightGBM 7 leaves. No class weights, parameter search, checkpoint downloads or large neural training. Fit each family in its own subprocess with two threads to avoid OpenMP conflicts. Neural architectures remain justified alternatives only if these baselines leave a demonstrated modeling gap worth their data and compute costs.

Fit a logistic probability calibrator on 2021 predictions only. Persistence and rainfall use the same calibration period. Set alert thresholds on pooled 2022 selection rows at budgets of 1 and 5 false alerts per 100 decisions. Include ties as complete groups; choose maximum-recall feasible threshold, with fewer false alerts as tie-break. Freeze thresholds before tests. Report realized test budgets, never adjust thresholds to enforce a test budget.

Select the maximum equally weighted development/geographic-validation AP, breaking ties by mean Brier, then name. Freeze every fitted recipe, calibration, threshold and selection record before final evaluation. Report all predeclared models on the same final rows once. No test-based reselection. A disappointing result is a result, not permission to tune.

## Evaluation, uncertainty and stop rules

Report prevalence, AP, Brier, log loss, reliability bins, precision, recall and false alerts per 100 decisions at both frozen thresholds. Report per-gauge and HUC2 performance and macro gauge AP, omitting undefined single-class APs explicitly. Distinguish high-flow days from episodes, defined by at least seven intervening accepted below-threshold days; missing days break episode certification. Report onset detection for certified episodes with a complete preceding seven-day history. Warning lead time is issue to the target local day's start, not an inferred instantaneous crossing.

Estimate paired AP and recall differences for the selected candidate against persistence and the strongest weather+hydrology candidate using 500 seeded bootstrap resamples in each test: HUC4 clusters, and calendar-month blocks separately. These are two sensitivity intervals, not proof that all spatial and temporal dependence vanished. An incremental-value claim requires positive lower AP bounds under both bootstraps on both forward and geographic tests, no Brier regression over .005, and no recall loss at the frozen 1% budget. Also report memory ablations and regional regressions. At least 20 certified episodes and five gauges per final slice are required for a substantive test claim; otherwise report insufficiency.

Even a statistical gain does not clear the historical-publication, flood-impact-label or prospective-validation gates. Keep all candidates isolated. Preserve production artifacts by hashes and never write persistent application data.

Download bounded Zarr chunks rather than the 10 GB forecast or 29 GB Caravan bundles. Pin `numcodecs==0.16.5` as a research-only dependency. Keep new source cache below 500 MB, worker RSS below 6 GB, and one model worker active at a time. Stop an impractical run before swapping. If a meaningful experiment is conclusive, do not add architectures merely to fill the list. Run focused tests and the complete Python suite, document source and code hashes, runtime and memory, and commit the verified result.
