# Hourly high-flow onset experiment

## Frozen question and scope

Does recent measured river dynamics improve 6, 12 and 24-hour onset warnings over flow proximity and physical extrapolation, including at previously unused gauges? This isolated experiment supersedes the unexecuted acquisition assumption in `HYDROLOGY_SPRINT_PROTOCOL.md`. Sandbox DNS fails, but approved network commands reach the current USGS OGC API. The legacy NWIS pilot returned an empty body. No production code or model will change during this study.

The target is hourly instantaneous discharge exceeding the gauge's 2000-2015 daily-mean discharge 95th percentile after 24 consecutive accepted hourly readings at or below that threshold. Equality is not an exceedance. This is high flow, not verified flooding or an hourly 95th percentile. Discharge needs no stage-datum conversion. Flood-stage targets remain inappropriate without historical threshold and datum evidence.

## Data and immutable split

Use official USGS OGC v1 `daily` and `continuous` collections, discharge parameter 00060, daily-mean statistic 00003 or instantaneous statistic 00011, cubic feet per second, approved values only, with no qualifier. Reject ambiguous series, duplicate timestamps, unexpected units and pagination anomalies. Keep compressed responses, exact URLs, retrieval times and hashes. Quarterly continuous requests bound resource use; two download workers and two numerical threads fit the 18 GB machine. Exclude gauges without 3,000 accepted reference days or positive reference discharge. Exclusions must be recorded, never replaced after viewing outcomes.

Reuse the existing metadata registry for development. Within each HUC2 choose the first stable SHA256 ordering of `shift42` plus station ID. Select two gauges in 01, 02, 03, 05, 07 and 12, one in other non-western regions. Roles remain development or geographic validation as previously assigned. Reserve up to three previously unused gauges in distinct HUC4s in each of 17 and 18 from the full CAMELS metadata registry. Exclude station IDs listed by any previous station registry before freezing. Every western CAMELS HUC4 has already appeared in a previous reservation, so this is not a new regional-climate test. Choose by the same hash, one gauge per HUC4, with drainage area between 20 and 1,200 square miles and known elevation. A HUC4 screen is not proof of independent drainage polygons; distinct HUC2s separate fitted and final geographic groups. The gauges and 2025 discharge outcomes are new, but their regions and possibly nested catchments appeared in earlier studies. They are hydrologically disjoint from this experiment's fitted HUC2s. This is not ungauged forecasting: local history is an input.

Fit only development gauges in 2021-2022. Fit probability calibrators only on development gauges in 2023. Select models and alert thresholds using 2024 development and geographic-validation gauges, weighting their average precision equally. Geographic-validation observations from 2023 supply feature warmup only. Never fit a transform on geographic-validation or final-geographic data. Purge decisions whose 24-hour labels cross a calendar-year boundary. Evaluate once in 2025, separately on development geography and reserved geographic-test catchments. This is a fresh USGS discharge-outcome year relative to the retained hydrology experiments; other earlier hazard studies have inspected different 2025 outcomes.

Do not acquire final 2025 observations until model, calibrator and threshold hashes are locked in `selection.json`. Reference climatology from final geography is allowed before this lock. Keep final-geographic observations out of all development fitting and selection.

## Timing, features and labels

Right-closed hourly bins retain the last accepted reading in the preceding hour without forward filling or interpolation. Decisions occur at 00, 06, 12 and 18 UTC. Use observations only through issue time minus two hours. Require 169 consecutive accepted hourly values through that cutoff and 24 quiet hours through the same cutoff. Do not use quiet conditions through issue time for eligibility. A crossing in the two-hour blind interval is not removed retrospectively. Forecast labels cover `(issue, issue + horizon]`; require all following 24 hours observed for a common comparison population. An onset needs 24 quiet hours, so at most one certified onset can occur in a 24-hour target interval. A previous onset can be in progress at issuance and must not be relabelled as a future onset.

Features are log1p discharge divided by the historical reference at lags 2, 8, 14, 26, 50, 74 and 170 hours; corresponding multiscale changes, six-hour acceleration, 24/72-hour range, annual phase, latitude, longitude, drainage area and elevation. These are causal hydrological representations, not a measured soil-water balance. Compare a daily-delayed feature ablation with the recent dynamics. Actual receipt timestamps and unrevised historical telemetry are unavailable: the two-hour delay is an explicit assumption, not demonstrated as-issued availability. Approved records are retrospective. No archived weather run in the existing cache covers this entire fresh test, so do not substitute reanalysis or a retrospective forecast.

## Frozen candidates and selection

At each horizon compare calibrated flow proximity, linear six-hour trend projection and damped trend projection (six-hour decay time), seasonal climatology, standardized logistic regression, LightGBM delayed flow (lags 74/170 and static features), LightGBM current flow (lag 2 and static), LightGBM multiscale dynamics, XGBoost multiscale dynamics, and a physical-residual LightGBM regression. Residual regression predicts the maximum future normalized log flow minus the damped projection; threshold distance is a ranking signal, then calibrated. All learned models fit identical rows. Do not tune a hyperparameter search: logistic C=1, tree depth/leaves and 250 iterations fixed in code before fitting. Calibrate all ranking scores with one-variable logistic regression on 2023 only. A negative calibrator slope fails that candidate rather than reversing its interpretation. Fit each boosting family in a separate process. Do not force a neural model unless this comparison leaves a specific unresolved representation failure.

For each candidate choose a single threshold on pooled 2024 validation rows maximizing true positives under one false alert per 100 decisions, including tied scores together. Candidate ranking is equal-weight forward/geographic-validation AP, then Brier score, then name. Select the strongest non-baseline and strongest physical/statistical baseline before any final outcomes. Freeze every artifact and threshold. Evaluate every frozen candidate once in 2025 for transparent ablations; only the selected candidate is eligible for a promotion claim. No test-oracle threshold or post-test model selection.

## Operational metrics and promotion gates

Report AP, Brier score, probability reliability bins, row precision and recall, false alerts per 100 decisions and per station-day, false-warning episodes separated by more than 24 hours, unique eligible event recall, first-warning lead, and regional/station results. Also report all certified raw onsets, how many have any eligible prior decision per horizon, and losses from missing history or the observation buffer. Conditional median lead alone can improve by missing difficult events; compare lead on jointly detected events. Use paired HUC4 cluster bootstrap with 500 fixed-seed draws for AP, event recall and false-alert differences, disclosing limited clusters and single-class draws. These intervals do not address every shared-weather dependence.

Promotion requires all of: at least 20 eligible events in each final partition and 50 in total at the evaluated horizon; AP improvement with paired 95% lower bound above zero in both partitions; event recall gain of at least five percentage points in both partitions with pooled paired lower bound above zero; Brier score no worse in either partition; no final HUC2 above three false alerts per 100 decisions and pooled burden at most 1.5; and no loss of median lead on jointly detected events. At least one horizon must pass every statistical gate. Operational integration additionally requires historical input-availability evidence and a site-to-gauge applicability assessment, both unavailable at freeze time. Success on retrospective approved measurements alone cannot clear that final gate. Do not relax failed gates or replace a selected candidate after test inspection.

## Reproduction and interpretation

Run the `freeze`, `development`, then worker fitting commands, lock selection, then `test` acquisition and final evaluation. Source files, dataset hashes, candidate artifacts, predictions, per-gauge coverage and machine-readable gate decisions remain under `experiments/sprint-20261017-shift-hydrology`. Later results documentation must give exact commands and findings. Preserve all earlier artifacts and `models/` hashes. A failed experiment is a result, not permission to promote a more complicated model.

Primary sources: USGS OGC documentation at `https://api.waterdata.usgs.gov/docs/ogcapi` and continuous schema at `https://api.waterdata.usgs.gov/ogcapi/v1/collections/continuous/schema`. The multiscale representation follows the computational motivation in Gauch et al., *Rainfall-Runoff Prediction at Multiple Timescales with a Single Long Short-Term Memory Network*, `https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.07921`; this experiment does not implement their architecture or claim their results. Lees et al.'s ungauged-basin study, `https://doi.org/10.5194/hess-27-139-2023`, does not justify calling local-flow-conditioned predictions ungauged forecasts.
